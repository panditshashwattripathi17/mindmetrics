Cognitive Fatigue & Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman

Kahneman describes two modes of thinking. System 1 is fast, automatic and intuitive. System 2 is slow, deliberate and effortful. Solving a calculus problem or tracing a bug in Python needs System 2, and System 2 gets tired.

When your mind is overloaded, you fall back on quick gut reactions instead of careful reasoning.

What this means during exams

  • After hours of non-stop studying, logical reasoning gets worse and careless mistakes go up.
  • Stress pushes us toward System 1 shortcuts, such as guessing or panicking.
  • Short breaks protect System 2, which is why the focus timer on this site works.

Try this: do your hardest subject first, when your mind is fresh, and use the Focus Timer to take breaks before you hit a wall.

The Scout Mindset & Honest Self-Assessment

Julia Galef

Galef contrasts the soldier mindset, which defends what we already believe, with the scout mindset, which tries to see things as they really are. A scout wants an accurate map, even when it is uncomfortable.

Seeing clearly is more useful than feeling reassured.

What this means for burnout

  • Soldier thinking says, "I'm fine, I can pull another all-nighter."
  • Scout thinking asks, "Are my sleep, mood and focus actually getting worse?"
  • Logging real numbers each day gives you data instead of guesses.

Try this: do a quick daily check-in and compare your weekly and monthly trends on the Dashboard. Treat it as information, not as a judgment of yourself.
